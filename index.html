The Cost of Inaction

As the planet warms, the economic damage from local climate disasters is not rising gently — it is accelerating. Scroll to follow the link between global temperature and the bill your region is being handed.

Scroll to begin ↓

1 · The Baseline

For the first half of the 20th century, the global temperature anomaly sat comfortably below the long-term average. This is our baseline: a relatively stable climate, and a world that recorded comparatively few billion-dollar weather disasters.

Temperature anomaly, earliest reliable records through 1950.

2 · The Shift

From the 1950s onward the line begins its relentless climb. Each decade is warmer than the last. The curve that once hugged the baseline now bends sharply upward — the unmistakable signature of a warming planet.

Watch the anomaly cross from negative to positive.

3 · Emergence of Extremes

Warming is not abstract — it lands somewhere. Here is the world map of recorded climate disasters up to 1990. Each bubble is sized by the economic damage it caused and coloured by type: floods, storms, droughts, wildfires and more.

Bubble area = inflation-adjusted damage. Colour = disaster type.

4 · The Modern Crisis

Now extend the map through 2026. The bubbles swell and multiply across every inhabited continent. The same warming line from the opening scenes is now written across the globe as escalating, costly damage.

The map now spans the full record, 1900–2026.

5 · The Economic Toll

Aggregated by decade, the financial story is stark. Total damages do not grow in a straight line — they grow exponentially, stacked ever higher by storms and floods. This is the cost being passed to taxpayers and municipal budgets.

Stacked by disaster type. Hover any segment for exact figures.

6 · Explore the Data

The trend is set — now the evidence is yours to interrogate. Drag to select a time range on the temperature chart: the map and the damage breakdown update instantly to that period. Hover any bubble or bar for precise numbers.
