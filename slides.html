The Problem

Climate damage is accelerating quickly

  • Climate & weather disasters used to be a line item. Now they reshape budgets.
  • The damage curve bends upward, decade after decade, far faster than population or inflation alone.
  • The bill lands locally: floods, storms, droughts, wildfires hit cities and regions first.

1900 → 2026, in numbers

$6.3T
Total adjusted damage
+1.2°C
Warming vs 1900s
4.6x
1980s → 2010s damage
~40s. Hook with the numbers card. $6.3 trillion in inflation-adjusted damage over the record; the planet is ~1.2°C warmer than the 1900s baseline; decade damage grew 4.6× from the 1980s to the 2010s. This is the gap we want a policymaker to feel.

The Data

Our Datasets

EM-DAT — disaster impacts

  • International Disaster Database (CRED / UCLouvain)
  • Filtered to climate & weather events only (hydro / meteo / climatological)
  • Inflation-adjusted damage, event counts, region, type, year

~1,850 aggregated records · 1900–2026

Global temperature anomaly

  • Observed monthly anomalies → annual mean (1940+)
  • Pre-1940 back-filled from NASA GISTEMP, rebased to the observed series
  • 127 annual points · 1900–2026

Both preprocessed in Python → lightweight JSON

~35s. Why two datasets: one for the cause (temperature), one for the consequence (cost). Stress the climate-only filter — that's a design decision that keeps the correlation legitimate, and it answers a likely Q&A question.

Audience & Goal

For municipal policymakers

Who

City & regional decision-makers who set budgets and plan infrastructure, a specialized audience that can act on detail.

The action we want

Move climate adaptation up the budget agenda now, frame inaction as a compounding cost, not a distant risk.

How we frame it

  • Guided story first: build the cause → consequence argument step by step.
  • Then hand over control: let them interrogate their own region / time range.
  • Concrete money figures, not abstractions.
~30s. This directly addresses the Assignment-2 feedback: name the audience and the action. Goal = provoke budget prioritization of adaptation. End of elevator pitch (~2 min). Hand to demo presenter.
